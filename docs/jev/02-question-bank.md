# JEV question bank

Use these questions in interviews, call reviews, and funnel reviews. Ask the first five before explaining Nebula Ideas' solution. Record short, anonymized evidence; do not copy unnecessary personal data.

## 1. Trigger

- What changed recently that made this problem worth addressing?
- What were you trying to improve: IT services, product capability, operations, architecture, process, or something else?
- Was AI one of the options you considered, or was it the starting assumption?
- How urgent is this now: exploration, planning, funded initiative, or active delivery problem?

## 2. Current state

- What is happening today?
- What have you already tried?
- Why have existing solutions, vendors, consultants, or internal teams not solved it?
- Where does the work slow down, create risk, or make decisions harder?

## 3. Consequence

- What does the problem cost today in money, time, risk, missed opportunity, or team friction?
- What happens if nothing changes in the next 6–12 months?
- Which business or technical outcome would make the effort worthwhile?

## 4. Decision and qualification

- Who owns the problem?
- Who approves spend or implementation?
- Which organization size, industry, or operating context is involved?
- Is the problem important enough to discuss scope and a next step?
- Is budget a blocker now, later, or not a blocker at this stage?

A conversation is **qualified** when the organization fits a commercially viable target, has a real improvement problem, involves a decision-maker, is not blocked by budget, and the discussion is concrete enough to discuss scope or a next step.

## 5. Proof and offer

- What would you need to see before paying for an assessment?
- Which output would be most useful: roadmap, cost/time case, risk view, AI opportunity view, or alignment workshop?
- What would make a fixed-scope Clarity Sprint feel safer than open-ended consulting?
- What would make you decline the Sprint even if the diagnosis sounds useful?
- What would the Sprint need to deliver for a paid follow-on engagement to be justified?

## 6. Message comprehension

Show the current message without selling it:

> AI is an amplifier. Understand before you amplify.

Ask:

- What do you think Nebula Ideas does?
- Who do you think this is for?
- What would you expect to receive?
- Does “amplifier” sound useful, vague, or unrelated to your problem?
- What would you expect a Clarity Sprint to cost or take?

Do not ask whether they like the wording until after they explain their understanding.

## 7. Market and language

- Where is the organization located?
- Which language do they prefer for the conversation and written deliverable?
- Was the visit in English or Spanish?
- Did the language change the problem they described, the examples they used, or only the reading experience?

Language is a baseline proxy, not a geography conclusion.

## 8. Evidence review

After each cycle, answer:

- Which language produced more qualified conversations, not merely more bookings?
- Which problem wording attracted the right organization?
- Which buyer role was overrepresented?
- Which CTA placement produced qualified intent?
- What did buyers ask that the current page does not answer?
- Did anyone pay for the Sprint? What did they buy?
